## The challenge

MobileIron was entering a crowded market with genuinely superior technology, and nobody knew it. When it launched new phishing protections, it was going head-to-head with Google and Proofpoint — household names in enterprise security. The problem wasn't the product. MobileIron Threat Defense protected against the new generation of phishing attacks that everyone else missed: QR codes, NFC tags, and malicious links delivered via social media and messaging apps. The problem was cut-through.

A press release announcing better phishing protection would disappear into the noise. The campaign needed to do something journalists would actually talk about — and ideally, something that made the point without saying a word.

## What I did

I led strategy, creative direction, and attribution for a campaign that turned the threat into the message.

We went phishing ourselves.

Journalists, analysts, and influencers received hand-delivered boxes of custom-baked cookies — each one iced with a QR code and nothing else. No explanation. No brand message. Scan the cookie, find out what happens. The QR code led to a landing page that reframed the moment: *"Uh oh. Your phishing defences have crumbled."* — and introduced MobileIron Threat Defense as the solution for the attacks nobody else was protecting against.

The campaign paired the stunt with a targeted media relations programme, placing the story in key business, technology and security publications to ensure the moment had legs beyond the initial delivery.

## The results

- **97% QR scan rate** — an overwhelming majority of recipients scanned immediately
- **10+ media placements** in business, tech and security publications, including HelpNet Security, Threat Post, and Healthcare Global
- **25% increase in product trials** during the campaign period — the highest in the quarter
- **43% increase in Annual Recurring Revenue pipeline** versus the prior period
- **Gold, NYX Marcom Awards 2021**
- **Sabre Award 2021**

The campaign demonstrated that the most effective way to explain a threat is to make people feel it — and that a creative idea executed with precision can move commercial metrics, not just earn coverage.
